The Aral Sea was once the fourth-largest lake in the world, covering roughly 68,000 square kilometers between Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. In the 1960s, Soviet planners diverted the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers to irrigate cotton fields across the arid plains of Central Asia. The engineering worked exactly as intended: cotton production surged, and Uzbekistan became one of the world's largest exporters. What the planners did not account for was that the two rivers were the lake's only meaningful inflow. Within three decades the shoreline had retreated by more than a hundred kilometers, stranding fishing fleets in what is now desert.

Desalination offers one partial answer to regional water scarcity, though it comes with its own arithmetic. Reverse osmosis plants consume between three and four kilowatt-hours per cubic meter of freshwater produced, which makes the process viable mainly where energy is cheap or seawater is close at hand. Israel now sources roughly 85 percent of its municipal water from desalination, and the marginal cost has fallen below 60 cents per cubic meter at the largest facilities. The brine discharge remains the unresolved problem: for every liter of drinking water, the plant returns about 1.5 liters of concentrated salt solution to the sea, altering the local ecology in ways that are still poorly measured.

Language models handle long documents by splitting them into chunks, and the split points matter more than most practitioners expect. A fixed-width strategy that cuts every 512 tokens will routinely sever a sentence from the clause that qualifies it, producing embeddings that represent half an idea. Recursive character splitting improves on this by preferring paragraph breaks, then sentence breaks, then whitespace, degrading gracefully as the text resists clean division. Semantic chunking goes further, embedding each sentence and cutting where consecutive similarity drops below a threshold — expensive at index time, but it tends to preserve argumentative units that a naive splitter would shred.
